# QuantLab Apple-inspired design pass

The brief was to strengthen Apple-like polish while keeping the beginner-friendly flow and honest probability explanations. The implementation borrows design principles, not branding or assets.

## Sources reviewed

- https://www.apple.com/ — short product stories and distinct calls to action.
- https://www.apple.com/iphone/ — strong type hierarchy and spacious product presentation; visually inspected in browser.
- https://www.apple.com/os/ios/ — capability-first product language and OS presentation.
- https://www.apple.com/macbook-air/ — focused product headline and progressive detail.
- https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography — readable hierarchy and limited typefaces.
- https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/layout — aligned groups, adaptable layouts and progressive disclosure.
- https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials — navigation/control materials separated from content.
- https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2017/01/behind-apples-new-campaign-one-night-on-iphone-7/ — historical product demonstration through the work people make with it.

Primary Apple pages were accessed directly through web/browser tools. The HIG text endpoint failed; browser rendering provided the source. No local crawler was needed for these known URLs. This is a bounded reference study, not a claim to have reviewed every Apple product or campaign.

## Applied decisions

One centered workspace replaces the sidebar. The three model probabilities are the visual focus. A compact sticky navigation layer is the only translucent surface. Type size, whitespace and weight create hierarchy; exact technical content remains optional. No Apple logo, proprietary font, marketing image or copied slogan was added.

Navigation now moves to the start of the selected page, and invalid settings open their enclosing disclosures before native error focus. Calculations and evaluation limits remain unchanged.
