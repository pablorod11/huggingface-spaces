      Would trading costs change the result?
      

      Imagine buying, then selling. A price rise can leave you with less than you expected once costs are paid. Try it with pretend money.
      

      This is a separate math story. It does not use your forecasts or price file. You choose how many trades finish; the app does not predict fills. Results show gains or losses, not your account balance.
      

      Change the costs
      All amounts use the same pretend units. The price gap is charged once per finished trade. Fees and extra price costs are charged twice: once to buy and once to sell. Waiting only makes the price worse in this story. Keep its cost separate from the extra price cost so you do not count the same effect twice. For every percentage here, 0.10 means 0.10%.
      

      

      How this story works
      Each trade uses the same starting amount. Every finished trade buys and sells once, with the same price change and costs. Unfinished trades are assumed never to fill, so they cost nothing here. All percentages are applied to the starting amount; money is not reinvested. Skipping a losing trade can help. Real orders can fill only partly, and their timing and price can differ.
      This is arithmetic, not a historical trading test, order-book replay or estimate of future profit. HftBacktest, NautilusTrader and KalshiMarketMaker are not running here.
      

      What if prices took surprise jumps?
      Make a pretend world: prices wiggle and sometimes jump. You choose all the settings. This does not learn from your price file or predict real markets.
      Build a pretend year
      This pretend year has 252 equal price steps. For the log inputs, −0.02 is about a 1.98% drop and −0.08 is about a 7.69% drop. The growth setting includes the jumps. Over one year the average price change is exp(growth setting) − 1; 0.06 gives about 6.18%.

      Rules and calculation checks
      

      A quick guide.
      QuantLab is a learning tool. It makes uncertain guesses about the next price, then checks earlier guesses against answers.
      Try it in three steps
      1. Open Price explorer. Keep Made-up prices selected.
      2. Press Show my guess. The three numbers show the chances of all three outcomes.
      3. Read Did the guesses help? A clever method is only useful if it beats a simple comparison.
      What do the words mean?
      Chance: how often something might happen in many similar situations. A 20% chance means about 20 out of 100, not a promise about one outcome.
      Guessing method: a set of rules that learns from earlier prices. Researchers call it a model.
      Practice and check: learn from earlier answers, then check on later answers that were kept apart. Looking at the check answers again and again can still make the test unfair.
      Made-up prices: a repeatable example, not real market data. Doing well on it does not show that a method works in real markets.
      Find your way around
      Price explorer makes the three guesses. Number games has separate calculators, including a pretend trading-cost story. What if? lets you invent a world with surprise price jumps. The arrows beside extra details open them when you want to learn more.
      Your file stays in this browser
      Your CSV is read here and is not sent to a modeling server. A few recent calculations are kept temporarily in this tab. Use Clear my results or reload to remove the app’s saved data. The browser controls when memory is actually reclaimed.
      These guesses can be wrong. They are not instructions to buy, sell or place a bet. This app has not shown that its guesses can make money.
      More detail · methods, privacy and limits
      Data cleaning & privacy
      CSV parsing is bounded to 2 MiB, 2,500 price rows, 64 columns and 16,384 characters per field. We normalize header case, trim surrounding date/price whitespace and accept an initial byte-order mark. Every change is counted in the report. Duplicate dates, missing values and invalid prices are rejected with row numbers. Large moves, unchanged prices and calendar gaps are flagged and retained. We cannot verify whether prices are adjusted from two columns alone.
      Temporary caching
      Identical validated dates, prices, validation approach and model revision can reuse one of up to three fitted runs in this tab for ten minutes from computation. Cache hits are labeled. No uploaded prices or reports are written to localStorage, IndexedDB or a service-worker cache. Reloading or Clear my results removes application-held data; browser and operating-system memory reclamation is outside the app’s control. Public app assets may be cached by your browser or Hugging Face. The saved Python benchmark is fetched with revalidation.
      Validation contract
      At close t, returns, momentum, realized volatility and drawdown use only observations through t. Strict mode fits at least 500 training rows, temperatures on 150 later rows, and model choice on another 150 rows. A one-row embargo separates each boundary, followed by a reserved final 200-outcome evaluation. The coefficients, temperatures and model choice remain frozen for the latest forecast; features and EWMA state advance causally. Classic mode uses expanding training, a shared 150-row calibration/selection block and test blocks of up to 200. Repeatedly inspecting a reserved evaluation can still overfit it. This is not a permanently sealed holdout. Never tune using the test charts. Strict mode also lets unadjusted past-outcome counts compete during selection, with no temperature fitted for that candidate.
      The target is the next observed close’s log return. Downside is below −0.02 log return, approximately −1.98% simple return; the middle event includes both boundaries, and the final event is a positive return. Probabilities sum to one.
      EWMA challenger
      The ridge mean stays frozen within each fold. Residual variance updates online with a fixed 20-observation half-life, using only outcomes already known before each forecast. Its own target never updates its own variance. The audit records volatility and timing; annualization assumes 252 equal-length observations and is only illustrative for gapped CSVs.
      Two distinct execution modes
      Browser experiment: empirical frequencies (untuned and temperature-adjusted in strict mode), ridge with Gaussian residual probabilities, EWMA residual-volatility probabilities, and multinomial logistic regression are fitted to the selected data. Synthetic JavaScript draws differ from Python even with the same seed.
      Precomputed Python benchmark: a saved 900-observation synthetic run actually fitted scikit-learn, XGBoost and LightGBM, completed three training-only Optuna trials, computed SHAP and recorded MLflow metrics. Loading it does not execute those libraries. Download the full Python/Gradio project to run them locally.
      When the learning examples may not be enough
      A note appears when an outcome has fewer than 10 training examples, or when recent price patterns are unusual compared with training. These are simple checks, not a guarantee of reliability. The absence of a note does not prove a guess is safe or accurate.

      Implementation limits
      Classic calibration reuses data for temperatures and model choice. Strict mode separates these stages but cannot prevent repeated human inspection. Financial tails and nonstationarity can invalidate normal residual assumptions. Tiny test samples and sparse reliability bins are especially unreliable. The validation panel shows matched untuned and calibrated empirical baselines, fold populations, support warnings and descriptive paired uncertainty. It shows rolling loss, Brier score, entropy and event counts; it does not implement feature-drift testing or an automatic fallback strategy. The saved Python benchmark predates this panel and does not have row-matched baseline probabilities, so its new comparisons remain unavailable. SHAP explains uncalibrated raw class margins, not causal effects. Close-based information does not imply executable same-close fills. The separate trading-cost story uses chosen assumptions; it is not an economic backtest. No live trading is included.
      TFT, DeepSurv, PPO, calibrated rough-volatility models, SPDE solvers and quantum algorithms remain unimplemented. Qiskit has no defined task here; TensorFlow would duplicate the optional PyTorch backend. The downloadable project records all requested books and access limits; full books were not read.
      The implied-volatility solver and jump-mixture PDF/CDF explorer are independently implemented standard methods inspired by Quantflow. No upstream source is copied and the Python package is not installed in this static host.
